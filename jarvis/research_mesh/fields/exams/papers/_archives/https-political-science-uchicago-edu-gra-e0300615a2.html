Comparative Politics

Frequency and Structure of Examinations

The Comparative Politics (CP) Qualifying Exam occurs over two consecutive days, typically at the start of Fall Quarter. At the start of each day of the exam, students receive a set of questions corresponding to that day’s list. Students choose one question, write an essay in response, and return it to the exam administrator eight hours later. Each day’s essay should be between 15 and 25 double spaced pages in length, including footnotes but not bibliography, diagrams, or tables. While students are free to consult any written source, the text of the exam should be original, and standard rules against plagiarism apply. Both essays will be read and graded by two CP faculty members.

Students will need mastery of the Comparative Politics Core List and one elective list (of their choosing) specializing in one area within CP. Day 1 covers the Core list; Day 2 the specialized list. Currently, the lists are: Comparative Political Economy; Ideology and Culture; Institutions; Nations and Nationalism; Order and Violence; and Political Regimes and Transitions. Students should indicate to that year’s exam readers in advance which specialized list they elect to take, and may want to consult with readers about expectations and procedures.

To pass, students must demonstrate, at a minimum, a broad and deep understanding of the subfield: its central texts, debates, methods, and the connections among them.  Exam answers that fail to do so will receive a “not pass” grade. Conversely, answers demonstrating mastery and originality may receive a “high pass” grade. Successful students generally choose questions and craft answers that, between the two essays, display a broad and deep understanding of the literature, and avoid repeating content across the two essays.

In accordance with departmental policy, students who do not pass the exam in their first attempt may retake the exam at a subsequent date. If a student fails to pass the entire exam in two attempts, their retention in the PhD program will come under review.

Preparation

Students who wish to take the Comparative qualifying exam should consult with relevant faculty advisors about choosing among the topical lists. Students will be responsible for the Core List and one topical list.
