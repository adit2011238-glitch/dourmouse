Qualifying Exams in Physics and Astronomy Graduate Programs

Thank you to David Nidever at Montana State University for originally compiling this list. Please help keep it up-to-date by adding any new information.

Color-coding:

  •  No written qualifying exam
  •  Optional written qualifying exam (normally can take classes instead)
  •  Has written qualifying exam

Ordering is roughly from the US News & World Report Rankings of Best US University for Physics:

https://www.usnews.com/education/best-global-universities/united-states/physics

Of 73 university done so far:

  • No written qualifying exam     46
  • Optional written exam              4
  • Has written qualifying exam   47

15. Yale University

 Physics

  •  Has written qualifying exam, two attempts
  • Students will have two opportunities to pass each part of the Qualifying exam. If a student fails any part(s), then they only need to retake those part(s) in their next attempt. Students who do not pass all four parts of the Qualifying exam by the beginning of their second year will typically take an Oral exam after 2-3 months, only in the part(s) that they failed. Alternatively, students may also opt to retake those part(s) of the written Qualifying exam at the beginning of their third year.
  • After two failures (either (one written + one oral) or (one written + one written)), the whole faculty will discuss the particular student, to decide if they should be allowed to continue in the PhD program.

https://physics.yale.edu/academics/graduate-studies/graduate-student-handbook/qualifying-exam-1

21. Ohio State University

 Physics

  •  No written qualifying exam
  • There is a candidacy exam, similar to astronomy (see below)

https://physics.osu.edu/candidacy-exam-info

 Astronomy

  •  No written qualifying exam
  • There is a candidacy exam
  • The written portion of the exam has three components. The first is a summary of 3 papers in the chosen research field. The second is an order-of-magnitude problem of the student's own devising, of comparable difficulty to the problems done in order-of-magnitude class. The third is a summary of the field as a whole. The student has 2 weeks to complete the written exam once assigned.
  • The oral exam is based upon a research project undertaken in the first 2 years of graduate school, and is scheduled within one month of the written portion of the exam. The oral exam consists of two parts. The first is an approximately 40 minute presentation to the exam committee on the completed research project. The second is an approximately 1 hour free-form question/answer session where the student may be asked about their presentation, their research project, or general astronomy and astrophysics.

https://astronomy.osu.edu/grad/current-students/candidacy-exam
