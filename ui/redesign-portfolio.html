What changed vs today

Today

JetBrains Mono everywhere — even headers. One typeface, one weight, dense three-pane grid (268/1fr/300). Cyan text at 85% on near-black: low contrast, flat hierarchy, nothing breathes.

Tomorrow

Four-role type system (display / HUD / mono / body). Near-white readable text with cyan as the *accent*, not the body. Icon rail + command deck layout. Gold reserved for the reactor and "sir" moments.

Rules of the system

R1

One hex per hue

Depth from an 8-step alpha scale, never tints. Glow survives.

R2

Gold is the reactor

Boot, wordmark dot, "sir" moments, in-flight. Nowhere else.

R3

Readability first

Body text near-white; cyan only for marks, lines, and identity.

The // Voice Lab

A

Talk, nothing else

One screen, one mode: wake word → speak → it answers by voice. No typing, no routing. The whole dispatch answers with its voice.

B

Train recognition

Enroll your voice, tune the wake word, adapt to your accent. Confidence bars show what it actually heard — every segment becomes training data.

C

Fine-tune further

Rate every exchange GOOD / BAD. Corrections, new vocabulary, and voice samples queue into the SFT training pipeline — the Dell node is the compute target.

D

Wake-word stack

Local VAD → wake word → speech-to-text → intent → dispatch. The arc-reactor dial becomes a listening orb with a live transcript pane.
