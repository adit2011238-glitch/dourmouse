## Shared foundations (agreed once, used by every thread)

1. **Jobs.** Any task that needs more than one agent or more than a few steps becomes a job with an id, an owner question, a status, a step budget, a board (files and notes the agents share) and a log. Chat turns stay chat; research runs, security scans and browser tasks become jobs, so they are not squeezed into the 8-step chat budget. The goals engine (`goals` agent, `goals.db`) is the starting point.
2. **One library.** Every thread that stores knowledge (papers, pages, files, notes, findings) writes through T4's library API: `add(source, text, meta)`, `search(query, filters)`, `cite(passage_id)`. No thread builds its own store.
3. **Agent identity.** Every agent has a profile: name, job, tools, model, memory namespace, workspace folder, permissions, and a capability test. T3 defines the profile; the other threads add their agents through it.
4. **Design system.** T6 publishes tokens and components first. No thread builds new UI outside them.

## T1. Research: a real research tool

**Owner's method:** 1) understand the general context of the topic, 2) find relevant papers, read them, draw a conclusion from each, 3) define the research questions, goals and sub-questions, 4) cycle through these and present the answers concisely and clearly.

**What exists today**
- `dourmouse/research_pipeline/` (about 3,100 lines): plan, discover sources, extract evidence with a verbatim passage check, synthesize, detect contradictions, critique, follow up, experiments. The verbatim check is good and stays.
- The RESEARCH screen says 13 of 15 stages have a tool. Missing by its own text: source validation and statistics.
- The search underneath is weak: `web_search` is Wikipedia's API with Brave and DuckDuckGo page scraping as fallbacks (`general_roster.py` around line 620). There is no academic source at all: no arXiv, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar or Crossref.
- Runs happen inside chat turns, so a real research job hits the step budget.

**Target: the four stages as a job**
1. **Context brief.** Encyclopedia pass plus the two most cited review papers on the topic (OpenAlex works filtered to type review, sorted by citations). Output: a one-page brief with key terms, the main schools of thought, a timeline and the open debates. Stored in the library. The owner can read and correct it before stage 2.
2. **Papers, read and concluded.**
   - Find: OpenAlex for breadth, Semantic Scholar for the citation graph (references and citing papers), arXiv for preprints and PDFs. Deduplicate by DOI. Rank by relevance to the brief, citations per year, recency and venue. Throttle to about 1 request per second per source; get free keys where they raise limits (OpenAlex now gives keyless use a very small daily budget).
   - Read: fetch the open-access PDF (arXiv, or the OpenAlex open-access location), extract text (the existing `extract_pdf` tool), split into sections.
   - Conclude: one **paper card** per paper: question, method, data or sample, main findings with quoted passages (verbatim-checked), limitations, how it bears on the topic, and a source grade (peer reviewed or preprint, citations, recency, agreement with others). The grade is the missing "source validation" stage.
3. **Questions.** After reading, not before: derive the research question, goals and three to six sub-questions from the gaps and disagreements in the cards. The owner edits and approves them in the UI before the cycle runs.
4. **Cycle and present.**
   - For each sub-question: retrieve from the cards, search again where evidence is thin (follow citations backwards and forwards), update the answer, record contradictions.
   - Stop when a cycle adds no new claim, or the job's budget is spent, and say which.
   - Present: one answer per sub-question in two or three sentences with claim-level citations and a confidence word, a short "where sources disagree" section, and a reference list in MLA or APA. Export to a Doc or Markdown.
   - Wire the existing `describe_samples` for numeric claims (the missing statistics stage).

**Acceptance**
- A benchmark of 10 fixed questions with known good sources. Measure citation precision (share of claims whose passage is found verbatim, target 95 percent or more), source recall against a reference list (target 70 percent), answers graded by the owner, time and cost per job.
- One end-to-end run through the real RESEARCH screen with screenshots, on a question from the owner's school work.

**Limits to state plainly:** paywalled papers are read from the abstract only, and the card says so. Free API limits mean a deep job can take tens of minutes; it runs in the background and reports progress.

**Decisions for the owner:** citation style (MLA, the report's style, or APA); whether to register free OpenAlex and Semantic Scholar keys (recommended).

## T2. Security: a McAfee-style guard for this Mac

**Owner's list:** monitor downloads and links and protect against them; monitor laptop and background activity for spyware; show what the school account can access; make sure no Wi-Fi router can see what the owner does; prevent and isolate dangerous downloads.

**What exists today** (`dourmouse/security/`, 22 modules, about 6,300 lines)
- Downloads assessment (`downloads.py`): real file type by bytes, quarantine flag, where it came from, signature and Gatekeeper verdict, double extensions, apps hidden in zips.
- Posture checks (`mac_detectors.py`): FileVault, SIP, Gatekeeper, stealth mode, Remote Login, Screen Sharing, Wi-Fi security. Monitoring indicators (`monitoring.py`), network change watcher (`netwatch.py`), lockdown blocklist (`lockdown.py`), browser history reader, AbuseIPDB reputation (needs a key), an AI analyst, a self-audit.
- Six high findings are open (SEC2-1, SEC2-2, SEC2-4, SEC2-6, SEC3-7, SEC3-9). The scanner cannot see root-owned listeners such as ports 22, 445 and 5900 (SEC3-7), and one bad launch-agent file stops every scan (SEC3-9). The application firewall on this Mac is off.

**Hard limit, said up front:** real-time interception (blocking a process the moment it starts, or a file the moment it is written) needs Apple's Endpoint Security entitlement, which Apple grants case by case through a provisioning profile; an unentitled client fails at start. So version 1 watches by polling and file-system events, which need no entitlement, and stops what it finds within seconds rather than before it runs. A signed system extension is a later step that needs a paid Apple developer account and Apple's approval.

**Build, in order**
1. **Fix the six open high findings first.** Their designs are already written in memory (`dourmouse_status_2026_10_31.md`).
2. **Downloads and files.**
   - SHA-256 every new file and check it against a hash reputation service (VirusTotal free key or MalwareBazaar). Run open YARA rule sets on executables, scripts, Office macros, PDFs and the contents of archives and disk images.
   - **Quarantine vault:** move a dangerous file into a Dourmouse vault folder, remove execute permission, keep the macOS quarantine flag, record why. It is released only by the owner.
3. **Links.**
   - Check every address before the browser pane loads it, and every link in mail the mail agent reads: Google Safe Browsing (free key), lookalike and punycode domains, newly registered domains.
   - A dangerous link gets a full-page warning in the pane, never a silent block.
4. **Spyware and background activity.**
   - Persistence: launch agents and daemons, login items, configuration profiles, system and kernel extensions, each with its signature and when it appeared.
   - Apps holding screen recording, accessibility, input monitoring, camera or microphone rights. This needs Full Disk Access granted to Dourmouse.app, to read the privacy database.
   - Unsigned or ad-hoc signed programs running from Downloads or temporary folders, and new outbound connections per process (the SEC3-7 netstat merge).
   - A known stalkerware list, and a diff against the baseline.
5. **School account.** One page that answers "what can my school see":
   - Whether any Mac management profile is installed (`profiles` command).
   - Whether a Chrome profile is managed by the school, with the policies and force-installed extensions it carries (Chrome's managed preferences).
   - Whether Chrome sync is on in that profile.
   - What Google says admins can see for a managed profile: profile and device reports, extensions and policies; live browser data only on Education Plus.
   - Advice that follows from the evidence, for example: keep school sign-in in its own Chrome profile and never sign the personal profile into the school account.
6. **Wi-Fi privacy.**
   - Say honestly what a router can see: always the addresses you connect to and how much traffic. It sees site names unless DNS is encrypted (DNS over HTTPS) and the browser uses Encrypted Client Hello. Chrome and Firefox use Encrypted Client Hello when encrypted DNS is on; Safari does not yet.
   - Check the current state (DNS encryption, a VPN or iCloud Private Relay active), offer an encrypted-DNS configuration profile the owner approves in System Settings, and recommend Private Relay or a trusted VPN to hide the addresses too. Never claim more than that.
7. **One status page.** Protected, needs attention or at risk per area, an incident timeline, the quarantine vault, and one-click fixes that ask for the admin password through macOS itself. Turning on the application firewall is the first fix.

**Acceptance:** a test pack of harmless samples (the EICAR test file, a fake double-extension app, a script that installs a launch agent in a test account, a lookalike-domain link), each detected, explained and quarantined or warned, with screenshots. The six high findings closed.

**Decisions for the owner:** grant Full Disk Access to Dourmouse.app; free keys for VirusTotal and Safe Browsing; whether to install the encrypted-DNS profile; whether to buy an Apple developer account later for real-time protection.

**Owner's ask:** every agent fully capable of something, genuinely useful and individual; a specific place, software or tool where they communicate, such as a separate tab; and a clear way to separate them.

**What exists today**

- No agent has its own memory or workspace, and none has a test proving what it can do.

**Target**
1. **Thirteen real agents** (the old names stay as aliases so nothing breaks):
   - Orchestrator (chief of staff; the companion becomes its friendly voice)
   - Researcher (research_info, evidence_pipeline, rnd, study, research_mesh)
   - Librarian (memory, device_wiki, admin_ops: the knowledge base and your files)
   - Mail and Calendar (mail, comms, scheduling, the mail parts of google_workspace)
   - Docs (docs, the Drive and Docs parts of google_workspace)
   - Browser
   - Mac Operator (system, apps, app_driver, compute)
   - Coder (dev_coding and reviewer; the five code backends become a model choice inside it)
   - Security
   - Markets (markets, forex, t212, mt5)
   - News and World (news, worldmonitor, globe)
   - Media (media, music, design_3d)
   - Planner (goals, tasks)
   - Agent Smith stays as the toolsmith, owner-gated.
2. **A profile for each:** a one-line job, a capability contract (what it promises), its tools, its model, its own memory namespace in the library, its own workspace folder, its permission profile, its step budget, and a **capability test** of 5 to 10 real tasks. The pass rate shows on its profile in the UI. An agent with no passing test is not shown as available.
3. **The Agent Room:** a real, persistent channel where agents talk.
   - Every job gets a thread. Messages are typed: ask, answer, hand-off, note, alert. Each message carries who sent it, what it cost, and links to the job's board.
   - Built on the existing bus and ask_agent, stored in SQLite, shown as its own tab in the app, and readable live.
   - The owner can post into any thread, pause a job, or take over.
   - The browser agent posts what it found on a page; the researcher reads it from the board. This is how agents share context instead of passing long text through prompts.
4. **Separation:** identity (persona and prompt), tool scope (already enforced), memory namespace, workspace folder, budget and permissions per agent. Risky agents (Browser, Mac Operator) can later move into their own process.

**Acceptance:** all 13 capability tests run live with a real model and the pass rates are recorded. A three-agent job (news, markets, Mac Operator) is shown end to end in the Agent Room with screenshots. Command Center lanes come only from real events (CC-1 fixed).

**Decisions for the owner:** approve the 13-agent roster (merges, not deletions); the Agent Room as its own tab.

## T4. Knowledge base (RAG) and connections

**What exists today:** several stores that do not agree.
- `memory_store.py`: SQLite full-text store.
- `memory_embed.py`: optional local Ollama embeddings, which conflicts with the cloud-only model policy.
- `global_memory.py`: a local embedding store.
- `shared_rag.py`: merges that store with the retired Windows desktop's vault.
- `desktop_rag.py`: about 940 lines that query the retired desktop over SSH, now dead code.
- `device_wiki`, `repo_index.py`, `semantic_graph.py` and the study folder.
- Retrieval accuracy has never been measured; the personal project report marks this criterion "partially met" for exactly that reason.

**Target: one library**
- **Storage:** one SQLite database in the workspace with a full-text index plus a vector index (sqlite-vec, or plain numpy below about 200,000 chunks).
- **Embeddings:** Gemini's embedding model through the existing key. File text leaving the Mac goes through the privacy gate: per-folder consent, Private mode excludes, the ledger records it.
- **Ingestion connectors:**
  - folders the owner picks: PDF, DOCX, text and Markdown;
  - scanned documents through Apple's on-device text recognition (Vision framework; no model leaves the Mac), which closes the report's media criterion;
  - Gmail with consent, saved browser pages, research paper cards (T1), notes vault, chat history.
  - Each chunk keeps its source, location, date and privacy tier.
- **Retrieval:** hybrid (keyword plus vector, fused), recency boost, filters by source and date, passage-level citations with the existing verbatim check.
- **Evaluation:** 50 questions over the owner's own documents with the passage that answers each. Report recall at 5 and mean reciprocal rank; target recall at 5 of 0.85. Re-run on every change.
- **Connections page:** one place that lists every connection (Google, Chrome, Spotify, brokers, keys), what each can read or do, when it was last used, and a disconnect button. Honest states only.
- **Remove dead code:** desktop_rag.py and the desktop branch of shared_rag.py (the desktop is retired, owner decision 2026-09-24).

**Acceptance:** the evaluation numbers recorded; a search in the app that shows passages with sources; old stores migrated, then retired.

**Decisions for the owner:** allow file text to go to Gemini for embeddings, per folder; which folders to index first.

## T5. Browser: signed in, with memory, part of the team

**Problems today**
- Google refuses sign-in in the Dourmouse pane. Google has blocked sign-in from embedded browser frameworks since January 2021, and the pane is an Electron BrowserView.
- The Chrome importer deliberately reads no cookies or saved passwords.
- The pane keeps no memory of pages for later, and its findings do not reach other agents.

**Options for signing in, and the recommendation**
- **A (recommended): a real Chrome profile as the signed-in engine.**
  - Dourmouse starts the owner's Google Chrome with its own "Dourmouse" profile (Chrome now requires a separate profile folder for remote control) and drives it over the DevTools protocol, the way `browser_agent.py` already drives Chrome.
  - The owner signs in to Google there once. Google accepts it because it is Chrome itself.
  - The window sits beside Dourmouse. The pane stays for quick, signed-out reading.
- **B: copy Google cookies from the owner's Chrome into the pane.** Needs a Keychain prompt and breaks whenever Google binds sessions to the device. Not recommended.
- **C: disguise the pane as Chrome.** Against Google's policy and fragile. Rejected.

**Context and teamwork**
- Every page the agent reads can be saved to the library (T4) with the agent's notes.
- Tabs are grouped per job.
- The browser agent posts what it found into the job's Agent Room thread (T3), so the researcher and others build on it.
- Before any page loads, the link check (T2) runs.

**Acceptance:** the owner signs in to Google once in the Dourmouse profile; the agent opens Gmail and Docs in it and reads a document on request; a research job reads three papers through it and the notes appear in the library and the Agent Room. Screenshots of each.

**Decisions for the owner:** approve option A; whether the Dourmouse Chrome profile may keep its own saved passwords (Chrome's own password manager).

## T6. UI: a real Mac app, not AI slop

**Owner's ask:** no gradients, no neon, no unnecessary subtext, no slop fonts. Research Apple's design rules, how Anthropic designs Claude, and how OpenAI designs ChatGPT; then plan and build a proper UI.

**What the research says** (sources at the end)
- **Apple (Human Interface Guidelines, macOS):**
  - Mac apps are built from a sidebar for navigation, content in the middle, and an optional inspector, with controls in the window toolbar.
  - Sidebar icons follow the accent colour the user picks, and the sidebar can be shown or hidden from the View menu.
  - In the 2025 design, toolbars and sidebars float on a glass material above the content to make the hierarchy clear.
  - Type is the system font (San Francisco).
  - Mac toolbar buttons stay visible when unavailable instead of disappearing.
- **Anthropic (Claude):**
  - warm neutral backgrounds and one restrained warm accent;
  - emphasis comes from typography and underlines, not colour or glow;
  - its own serif and sans families;
  - a clean, editorial page.
  - Third-party analyses are consistent on this; Anthropic has not published a design system.
- **OpenAI (ChatGPT):**
  - OpenAI Sans, a single in-house face, since the 2025 rebrand;
  - a calm, minimal, two-column interface: history on the left, conversation on the right;
  - no ornamental motion, heavy shadows or oversized decorative cards.
- **What all three share:**
  - content first, chrome quiet;
  - one accent used sparingly;
  - the platform's own or one deliberate typeface;
  - real controls, short labels;
  - no gradients, glows or explainer lines under every title.

**What is wrong with Dourmouse today**
- It imitates an operating system inside a window: painted traffic lights on every screen, a fake dock, a wallpaper and a fake menu bar.
- Space Grotesk and a mono face everywhere; a dark green theme with amber and neon accents.
- An explainer line under every title ("central agent dispatch", "real browser, shared with the AI").
- Uppercase labels throughout; 18 screens with overlapping jobs; status badges that are not true (CC-1).

**Direction: a native-feeling Mac app**
- **Window:** the real macOS window with its real traffic lights (`titleBarStyle: hiddenInset`), a sidebar with real vibrancy (Electron supports macOS vibrancy), and light and dark that follow the system.
- **Type:** the system font stack (San Francisco on the Mac) at macOS sizes: 13 px body, 11 px secondary, 15, 17 and 22 px headings. No display novelty faces, no mono except for code and numbers that line up.
- **Colour:** system neutrals plus the user's macOS accent colour, read through Electron. Semantic colours only for good, warning and critical. No gradients, no glow, no neon.
- **Layout:**
  - Sidebar: Chats, Agents, Research, Library, Browser, Security, Settings. Seven places instead of 18 screens.
  - Content in the middle; an inspector on the right for details, receipts and approvals.
  - A unified toolbar with search.
- **Behaviour:**
  - real menus and shortcuts (Cmd N new chat, Cmd K search, Cmd 1 to 7 for the sidebar places);
  - approvals as macOS-style sheets;
  - receipts in the inspector;
  - empty states that say what will appear, in one line.
- **Copy:** short labels in sentence case; no taglines; numbers and states shown, not described.

**Process**
1. A principles page and a token file (colours mapped to system colours, type scale, spacing on 4 and 8, radii, motion off by default except where it carries meaning).
2. A component sheet in the real Electron shell: sidebar list, toolbar, segmented control, table, list row, inspector, chat message, approval sheet, receipt, empty state.
3. Rebuild four screens first (Chats, Agents with the Agent Room, Research, Security), using real data from an isolated instance.
4. Owner review with real screenshots.
5. Then the rest, screen by screen, deleting the fake OS chrome, dock, wallpaper and retired screens.

The periodic-table idea from the last concept can live inside Agents as one view, drawn in this native style, if the owner wants it.

**Acceptance:**
- Before and after screenshots of every screen in light and dark.
- No gradient, glow or uppercase label left in the stylesheets (checked by a lint rule).
- Contrast at least 4.5:1 for text.
- Every action reachable from a menu or shortcut.

**Decisions for the owner:** approve the native-Mac direction; whether the warm, Claude-like neutrals or Apple's cool system neutrals should be the base.
